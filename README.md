# Knight of the Living Dead — Assignment 2

A 3D tower defense game: the dead rise from the graveyard and shamble down the cobbled road of a ruined medieval town, and three kinds of defenders on their towers have to stop every one of them before it reaches the town gate.

Unity 6000.6.6f1, URP, the new Input System. Inspired by Bloons TD 6 (towers as readable characters, rounds, an economy) and SAS: Zombie Assault (hordes of zombie types).

**Author:** Andrew Jiang. Video Game Design and Development (CSCI 381/780), Fall 2026, Assignment 2. Individual work.

**Running it:**

- **In the Editor:** open the project in Unity 6000.6.6f1, open `Assets/Scenes/Main.unity` and press Play.
- **Rebuilding the scene:** the scene, prefabs, materials, stats and waves are all generated by **Tower Defense > Build Greybox Scene** (`Assets/Scripts/Editor/GreyboxSceneBuilder.cs`). Running it again rebuilds them from the code and the models in `Assets/Models/`.

**For grading:** predictive aim can be compared against direct aim by setting `Tower.PredictiveAim = false` on a tower in Play mode. All tower, zombie and wave numbers are ScriptableObjects in `Assets/Data/` and can be changed in the Inspector.

## Controls

- **1 / 2 / 3** or click a tile in the build dock: choose the Knight Bastion, Archer Tower or Mage Spire. Click the selected card again to deselect.
- **Left-click a built tower** to open its upgrade panel. **Q** / **E** (or the buttons) buy the next tier on the left / right path; **Tab** (Shift+Tab backwards) or the arrows change its targeting; **Delete** or **Backspace** sells it for 70% of the gold spent on it.
- **Left-click** open ground: build the selected tower. The preview and its range ring turn green where you can build and red where you can't.
- **Right-click** or **Esc**: cancel, or close the upgrade panel.
- **Space** or the gold button: start the next wave.
- **F** or the 2× Speed switch: toggle 1x / 2x speed.
- **A** or the Auto-start switch: toggle auto-start. While it's on, the next wave starts by itself 2.5 s after the last zombie of a wave falls (the Start Wave button counts down), so you don't have to press Space each wave.
- **H**: hide or show the How to Build card.
- **R** or Play Again: restart after the game ends.

## Required features

| Requirement | Where |
|---|---|
| 3D physics | Projectiles are `Rigidbody` bodies launched with a velocity (`Projectile.Launch`) and damage what they collide with (`Projectile.OnCollisionEnter`, continuous collision detection). Zombies are kinematic bodies moved with `Rigidbody.MovePosition`, each with a capsule collider fitted to its model. Splash damage is a radius check around the impact point. |
| Placing towers | `TowerPlacer` raycasts the mouse onto the ground and checks the rules: a tower is selected, the game is running, under the cap, enough gold, at least 2.8 m from the road (`EnemyPath.DistanceTo`), 3.4 m from other towers, and clear of houses and other scenery (`Scenery` trigger colliders, so they block building but not shots or zombies). Every refusal shows a short message saying why. |
| Firing at a set rate | Each tower fires `shotsPerSecond` times per second at a zombie within its `range`, chosen by its targeting mode (`Tower.FindTargets`); the default, First, is the zombie furthest along the road, since one leak ends the game. |
| Predictive aim | `Ballistics.TryIntercept` solves the quadratic for where a straight-flying projectile at the tower's `projectileSpeed` meets a target moving at `Enemy.Velocity`, and aims there; if no interception exists it falls back to the target's position. The defender turns to face the aim point (yaw only, so it stays upright) and the shot is launched from its hand in 3D. `Enemy` carries its speed through corners so its velocity stays constant, which keeps the prediction accurate. |
| Game over on a leak | When a zombie reaches the last waypoint (the town gate), `GameManager.GameOver` stops time and the end screen appears. Surviving wave 20 calls `GameManager.Victory`. |

### Placing towers

The How to Build card in the top-left is on screen from the start:

1. Pick a tower: press **1 2 3** or click a card.
2. **Left-click** open ground. Green means OK.
3. **Right-click** or **Esc** to cancel.

It also says that towers can't go on the road, on houses or next to another tower, and that one zombie reaching the town gate loses the game. While a tower is selected a second line reads, for example, *Knight Bastion · left-click green ground · right-click to cancel*.

### The map

The dead rise inside a fenced graveyard in the top-left and leave through its iron gate. The road winds through the town in rounded bends (`RoundCorners` smooths each corner into an arc, and the road is one cobblestone mesh along it) and ends at a broken stone gatehouse in the town wall on the right. Ruined houses, carts, barrels, a well and dead trees line the edges; the pockets inside each bend are left open, so a tower there covers two or three stretches of road, as in Bloons. Lanterns along the road cast warm light. A darker earth verge runs under the cobbles so the road's edge reads cleanly from the camera, dead trees and old graves frame the outskirts, low mist drifts across the town and green wisps float over the graveyard. The ground runs well past the camera's view and fades into fog, so the map never ends at a visible edge. The layout is a table in the builder (`TownLayout`), so it's easy to change.

### Towers

Following Bloons TD 6, each tower is a character rather than a building: a chibi defender stands on top of a structure built to the tower's height and turns to face its target. Each has its own colour (Knight blue, Archer green, Mage purple), shown on its card and outline.

| Tower | Defender and attack | Cost | Fire rate | Projectile speed | Height | Range | Damage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Knight Bastion** | Knight in steel and blue on a stone bastion with a palisade; throws spinning axes | 100 | 2 / s | 14 | 2.5 m | 9 | 2 |
| **Archer Tower** | Hooded archer with a longbow on a tall watchtower | 150 | 1 / s | 35 | 7 m | 20 | 5 |
| **Mage Spire** | Bearded mage with a crystal staff on a rune-carved spire; casts orbs that splash for 2.5 m | 225 | 0.5 / s | 10 | 5 m | 13 | 8 |

The Knight is cheap, fast and short-ranged, the Archer is a long-range sniper that's hard to miss with, and the Mage is slow but hits groups. The Knight and Mage play an attack animation on every shot, and every defender kicks back slightly when it fires. The Knight's projectile is the same axe model he holds, tumbling end over end.

**Projectiles and effects** (`Projectile`, `Vfx`): every shot leaves a fading trail. The axe tumbles and bursts into splinters and dust; the arrow is a modelled shaft, steel head and fletching that sparks on impact; the orb is an emissive core with its own point light that blooms, and it explodes in a flash, a burst of motes and a shockwave ring as wide as its splash radius. Once a tower is upgraded, its trails and impacts take the colour of its leading path (fire orange, frost blue and so on). Building a tower kicks up dust. The effects are built in code from two generated soft-particle materials in `Assets/Resources/Vfx/`.

### Upgrades

As in Bloons TD 6, clicking a tower opens its upgrade panel. Each tower has **two paths of three tiers**. Either path can reach tier 3, but once one path passes tier 1 the other is capped at tier 1 (a 3/1 split), so every tower is a choice. Each path has a signature colour: the tower gets a glowing ring in that colour that thickens with each tier, the defender grows a little, tier 3 adds a swirling aura, and its shots take on the colour. Selling refunds 70% of everything spent on the tower.

| Tower | Path | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knight | **Berserker** (red) | Whetted Axes, 110: +1 damage | Battle Frenzy, 240: throws 50% faster | Whirlwind Warlord, 650: +3 damage, 50% faster, +2 range |
| Knight | **Axe Master** (gold) | Long Arm, 100: +2.5 range | Twin Throw, 260: a second axe at the next zombie | Axe Storm, 700: two more axes, each cuts through a zombie |
| Archer | **Marksman** (crimson) | Broadheads, 150: +3 damage | Piercing Shot, 280: arrows pass through 2 zombies, fly faster | Deadeye, 750: +12 damage, +2 pierce, +4 range |
| Archer | **Volley** (green) | Quick Draw, 140: 40% faster | Double Nock, 300: a second arrow | Arrow Rain, 720: two more arrows, 50% faster |
| Mage | **Pyromancer** (orange) | Wider Blast, 160: +1 m splash | Fireball, 340: +6 damage | Meteor, 850: +14 damage, +1.5 m splash |
| Mage | **Frost** (blue) | Chill, 150: hits slow zombies 30% for 1.5 s | Quick Cast, 300: 60% faster | Blizzard, 750: 55% slow for 2.5 s, faster, +1 m splash |

**Targeting.** Also as in Bloons, each tower has a targeting mode, shown in the upgrade panel and changed with **Tab** or its arrows:

| Mode | Shoots | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **First** (default) | the zombie furthest along the road | stopping leaks; the safe choice |
| **Last** | the zombie nearest the graveyard | thinning a wave early, e.g. a Mage at the start of the road |
| **Strong** | the zombie with the most HP left (ties: furthest along) | Archers on Clubbers, Revenants and the Zombie King |
| **Close** | the zombie nearest the tower | short-range Knights in a bend |

Extra shots go to the next zombies in the same order (for First, the next closest to the gate), or fan out if only one is in range; each is aimed with the same predictive intercept. Pierce lets a shot carry on through a zombie at full speed. Slowed zombies walk (and animate) slower, and the towers' predictive aim uses their slowed velocity. The Zombie King only takes half the slow. The paths are data on each `TowerStats` asset (`UpgradePath`, `UpgradeTier`), so a new upgrade is a new entry, not new code.

### Zombies

| Zombie | Speed | HP | Special | Gold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shambler** | 2.5 | 8 | The basic peasant zombie | 5 |
| **Runner** | 5.5 | 4 | Fast and fragile, sprints low to the ground | 3 |
| **Clubber** | 2 | 25 | A hulking brute with a spiked club and arrows in his back | 12 |
| **Plague Bloater** | 2 | 15 | Bursts into three Runners when killed | 8 |
| **Armored Revenant** | 2.2 | 30 | A rusted knight with sword and shield; every hit does 2 less damage (minimum 1) | 15 |
| **Zombie King** | 1.2 | 600 | The wave 20 boss, with a crown and skull scepter; roars when he appears | 200 |

**Health bars** (`HealthBar`): a small bar floats over each zombie and faces the camera. It stays hidden until the zombie is first hit, so a fresh horde stays uncluttered. It shades from green through yellow to red, a pale chip trails behind it to show the damage just taken, and its backing turns frosty blue while the zombie is slowed. The Zombie King's bar is wider and always shown.

Every zombie shares one look: chibi proportions, green skin, glowing yellow eyes and an exposed brain on the side of the head. They walk with their own animation (a lurching shamble, a frantic run or a heavy stomp) whose pace follows their speed, squash when hit, and play a death animation before the body disappears.

### Waves & economy

There are 20 waves, generated by the builder into `Assets/Data/Waves/` as `WaveDefinition` assets. Each wave is a set of groups (zombie type, count, spawn interval and start delay):

- Every wave has 4 + *n* Shamblers.
- Runners join from wave 3, Clubbers from wave 5, Plague Bloaters from wave 8 and Armored Revenants from wave 11, in growing numbers.
- Zombies spawn closer together as the waves go on (1.4 s apart at first, 0.6 s by the end).
- Wave 20 adds the Zombie King.

The player starts with 300 gold, earns gold for each kill and 40 + 10 × *n* for finishing wave *n*, and can have at most 10 towers. The next wave starts only when the player presses Start Wave, so there's always time to build, unless auto-start is on, in which case it follows 2.5 s after a wave is cleared (`WaveSpawner.AutoStart`). 2x speed and restart are always available.

### Art & animation

- **Characters:** the six zombies, three tower defenders and the hero (three armour levels; modelled but not yet in play) were generated with Hyper3D Rodin from text prompts, in a neutral A-pose with empty hands, then auto-rigged by Rodin with stock animations (walk, death, idle, attack and level-up). A Blender script (`ArtSource/rigging/process_apose.py`) fixes the head weights (chibi heads got bound to the shoulders), retints the zombies' skin to one shared green, trims clips, reduces each mesh to about 4,000 triangles after rigging (so the skinning survives) and exports to Unity.
- **Weapons** (axe, longbow, staff, sword, shield, club, scepter) are separate models held on the hand bones, so they move rigidly instead of stretching with the skin.
- **Buildings and props:** the three tower structures, two ruined houses, the town gate and wall, the cemetery gate, fence and gravestones, a well, a cart, a lantern, a dead tree and barrels, all generated with Rodin.
- **Ground:** cobblestone and grass textures from Poly Haven (CC0).
- **Lighting:** a warm dusk sun with soft shadows, fog, point lights in the lanterns, and a URP post-processing volume (bloom, ACES tonemapping, colour grading and a vignette) so the zombies' eyes and the Mage's crystals glow.
- `ProceduralAnimator` adds a squash on every hit, and a fallback waddle and topple for any model without a rig.

### UI

All of the HUD is built in code (`Hud.cs`) with rounded panels, a dark slate and gold palette, and TextMeshPro text:

- **Top bar:** gold, wave, towers placed out of 10 (red at the cap) and zombies destroyed.
- **How to Build card:** the numbered placement steps, always on screen until hidden with H.
- **Build dock:** one panel in the bottom-left, mirroring the wave controls on the right, with a compact tile per tower: a portrait of the defender (rendered by the builder into `Assets/UI/Portraits/`) with its hotkey badge, name, cost and stats. The selected tile gets an outline and tint in the tower's colour; tiles you can't afford dim and show the price in red.
- **Placement preview:** a see-through ghost of the actual tower (structure and defender) follows the cursor, tinted green where it can be built and red where it can't, with its range ring around it. A build hint sits above the dock, and a short message explains any refused placement or upgrade.
- **Upgrade panel:** opens on the right when a tower is clicked, with its live stats, its targeting mode, both paths (tier pips in the path colour, the next upgrade's name, effect and price, or LOCKED / MAXED) and a Sell button. The selected tower shows its range ring in gold.
- **Wave controls:** one panel in the bottom-right, level with the tower cards: two switch rows (2× Speed, Auto-start, each with its hotkey) beside a gold Start Wave button that shows how many zombies are left during a wave, or the auto-start countdown between waves.
- **Wave banner:** "WAVE 1 — THE DEAD ARE COMING" at the start, and a red "THE ZOMBIE KING RISES" warning on the last wave, on a dark band between gold rules so it stays readable over any part of the map.
- **End screen:** THE TOWN IS SAVED (gold) or THE TOWN HAS FALLEN (red), with a matching accent bar, waves survived, zombies destroyed and Play Again.

## Balancing

- **Towers trade off against each other.** The Knight deals the most damage per second for its price (4 / s) but only reaches 9 m, so it belongs in the bends; the Archer reaches 20 m and its arrows fly at 35, so it rarely misses even fast Runners; the Mage's 8-damage splash is the answer to groups and Bloater bursts.
- **Each zombie tests something.** Runners punish slow projectiles (predictive aim matters most against them), Clubbers soak damage, Bloaters turn one kill into three fast zombies near the end of the road, and the Revenant's armour makes the Knight's 2-damage axes nearly useless against him (1 damage per hit) while barely slowing the Archer and Mage.
- **The curve:** waves add a new type every few waves and spawn faster, and the King's 600 HP needs a mix of all three towers. Finishing a wave pays more as the game goes on (60 gold after wave 2, 240 after wave 20), so the player can keep building.
- **One leak loses**, as the assignment requires, so the game gives the player control: waves start only when they're ready, and the tower cap (10) is a hard limit separate from gold, so space and placement matter as much as money.

All of these values are on the ScriptableObjects in `Assets/Data/` and on `TowerPlacer` and `Economy`; the wave formulas are in `GreyboxSceneBuilder.BuildWaves`.
